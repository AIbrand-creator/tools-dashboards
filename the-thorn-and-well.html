      Where the chart does meet her words

      Hot Thirst and the cold

      Thirst can go out of balance when hydration is off. It shows up as overheating, getting chilled, sweating or big swings in temperature. The question is simple: is the body hot or cold right now, and what would bring it back?

      Hot Thirst does not mean making everything hot. The experiment covers hydration, body temperature, the temperature of the room or weather, the temperature of food and drink, and how dense the food is.

      Does cold exposure coincide with a broader loss of temperature and hydration regulation, and what reliably helps her return to baseline?

      “Cold = pain” is worth tracking alongside Thirst. The chart does not show that Thirst caused the pain.

      The regulation layer · Natural Shores, Tone 1 Smell

      Shores is regulated by thresholds

      Inside and outside, land and water, one room and another, one perspective and another. Natural Shores does not mean living by the sea. Tone 1 adds an instinctive trust or distrust signal: “something stinks here”, or “this place feels safe”.

      The working model: long displacement from a supportive environment → dysregulation → survival mode. Treat this as a body experiment. It does not prove the environment caused the condition.

      • The body comes alive in the right environment.
      • Light, a view or a threshold makes a difference.
      • Water or a change of density can restore her.
      • Crossing over, changing rooms or seeing from another side can move stuck energy.
      • Boundaries matter, because Shores is sensitive to the boundary line.
      • One fixed role or reality can feel dense and draining.
